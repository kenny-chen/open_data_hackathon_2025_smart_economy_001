大家好，我是艾娜。今日帶來——零售債券規劃工具。
主要受惠人有，食息一族、投資銀行、發債的政府企業。

現在市民普遍認知是：「庫房沒錢就發債。但有錢都拿去發工資，沒有幫助民生。」
因此，我帶來——零售債券規劃工具。

為什麼市民有這種認知？
第一，因為市民看不見，債務和民生幸福有什麼關係。而香港債務儀，用來分析債務、經濟增長、民生幸福之間的關係。
第二，因為大型基建太遠，太抽像；小型民生太高，沒人知。而GIS地圖，把財政地理可視化，讓大家看得見錢花在哪。
最後，需求預測模型，幫助發債人找出何時發債、發多少債、如何定價、哪類項目最吸引市民。
那麼發債，不再是為錢，而是為民生幸福。

我們用到了⾹港公開數據和私企數據，包括香港金融管理局、財經事務及庫務局、立法會發展事務委員會、國際貨幣基金組織、香港交易所等等。

第一，香港債務儀。
世界上所有發達國家都有債務，而香港的債務穩健，只佔GDP約42%，遠低於美國的120%與日本的250%。大家可以想像成，在香港今天賺一元，明天只需還4毛；但美國和日本，明天分別要還一塊二和二塊五，收入遠高於負債。
而將錢用於財政投資，像社會土木建設基金，幸福感才會長久。在2021至2023年，香港庫房因派糖虧損。這就像家庭一次性派錢買禮物，短暫開心，但無法持續；而在2025年，其實⽤好財政投資，香港庫房就收支平衡。

第二，GIS地圖。
全港十八區都有用到零售債券錢興建項目。而最多項目的地區是觀塘、西貢、離島、北區；單是四個地區，就合共有超過四十個項目。但為何市民看不見、感受不到幸福？
這是興建項目圖，橫軸是完成時間，最遠是2040年的古洞北供冷系統。直軸是開支，最高開支是啟德體育園，投資了超二百億港元。原來，小型民生僅佔不足十五%。市民自然覺得錢花得「太高太遠」。
那麼如何破題？就是以階段性成果，平衡大型基建與小型民生。這是我很深刻的民生幸福案例，觀塘翠屏河的天橋底，是起動九龍東的綠色活化項目。雖然這只是天橋底，但這是夏天陰涼位，下雨有頂蓋，讓一群附近工作的建築工人在這午休的地方。

第三，需求預測模型。
零售債券的成功，在於市民願意買多少。以2024年基建債券為例，只獲0.8倍認購；但2025年銀債，則超額認購1.8倍。「需求預測模型」正是回答這個問題。
模型有五個因素，包括人口基數、家庭存款、定價水位、承銷時機、項目類型。
其中，最重要是以「家庭存款」作為基礎財富指標，再以「定價水位」作為吸引力指標。
我們預測2026年零售債券可能達到1.2至2.1倍超額認購，金額介乎600至1000億港元。以最好情景為例，家庭存款按年上升2.2%，而定價水位在3.5釐。
每個家庭有多餘的生水，會選擇流向利息較高的零售債券水池。2024年12月，M2存款增長，即家庭少了生水買基債。相反，在2025年9月，M2存款和現金寶回報雙雙下降，就多了近400億生水買銀債。

民生就像煮一鍋湯，不只是加水加料，更要掌握火候與時間。
零售債券規劃工具是「火候計」，讓發債不再靠直覺，而是靠數據，煮出市民真正喝得出幸福的湯。

下一步，我們的工具不止分析債務，更幫助做出三個關鍵決策：如何比較不同經濟結構、如何平衡大型基建與小型民生、如何刺激零售債券市場需求。

Who benefits from retail bonds? People who earn interest, Investment banks, and Government and public companies that issue bonds.


💬 What do people think?
Many believe: “The government has no money, so it issues bonds. But even when it has money, it just pays salaries — nothing helps the public.”
That’s why we created this tool — to change that view.

Why do people feel this way?
1. They can’t see how debt connects to public happiness.
→ Our Debt Dashboard shows how debt, growth, and well-being are linked.
2. Big projects feel far away and unclear. Small ones are too few.
→ Our GIS Map shows where money is spent, making it easier to understand.
3. Bond issuers don’t know when or how much to issue, or what projects people like.
→ Our Demand Forecast Model helps answer these questions.
So now, issuing bonds isn’t just about raising money — it’s about improving lives.

📊 What data did we use?
We used public and private data from:

Hong Kong Monetary Authority
Financial Services and Treasury Bureau
Legislative Council
IMF
Hong Kong Stock Exchange
...and more.


🧮 Part 1: Debt Dashboard

All developed countries have debt. Hong Kong’s debt is 42% of GDP, much lower than: USA: 120% and Japan: 250%
Think of it like this: In Hong Kong, if you earn $1 today, you only owe $0.40 tomorrow. In the U.S., you owe $1.20. In Japan, $2.50.

Spending on public projects brings lasting happiness.
From 2021–2023, Hong Kong gave out cash (派糖), which caused losses.
It’s like buying gifts — fun for a moment, but not sustainable.
In 2025, smart investments helped balance the budget.

🗺️ Part 2: GIS Map
Retail bond money was used in all 18 districts.
The top 4: Kwun Tong, Sai Kung, Islands, Northern District — over 40 projects.
But why don’t people feel the benefits?

Look at the project chart: X-axis: Completion time (some as late as 2040), Y-axis: Spending (e.g., Kai Tak Sports Park cost over HK$20 billion)
Only 15% of spending went to small community projects. So people feel the money is spent on things that are “too big and too far.”

Solution: Balance big and small projects. Example: Under the flyover at Tsui Ping River in Kwun Tong — part of Kowloon East’s green revitalization. It’s shady in summer, covered when it rains — a great lunch spot for nearby workers.

📈 Part 3: Demand Forecast Model

Retail bonds succeed when people want to buy them. Example: 2024 Infrastructure Bond: Only 0.8× subscribed, 2025 Silver Bond: 1.8× oversubscribed.
Our model looks at 5 factors: Population, Household savings, Pricing level, Timing, Project type.

Most important: Household savings = basic wealth. Pricing level = attractiveness.
We predict: 2026 bonds may be 1.2× to 2.1× oversubscribed, Worth HK$60–100 billion. Best case: Savings grow 2.2%, Pricing at 3.5%.

When families have extra savings, they choose bonds with better interest.
In Dec 2024, savings grew — less money for infrastructure bonds.
In Sep 2025, savings and returns dropped — families bought HK$40 billion more silver bonds.

🍲 Final Message
Public well-being is like cooking soup.
It’s not just about adding ingredients — you need the right heat and timing.
Our tool is like a “heat timer” — it helps make smart, data-driven decisions.
So the “soup” brings real happiness to the people.

1. Data Loading & Preprocessing

Configuration: Sets up output directory, date range (from 2022-01-01), and defines 7 key financial variables

Multi-sheet Excel import: Reads data from 5 different sheets containing bond subscription, population, fund NAV, deposits, and HSI data

Time series preparation: Converts to datetime index and resamples all data to monthly end-of-month frequency

Data combination: Joins all datasets using outer join and applies forward fill to handle missing values

2. Stationarity Testing

ADF & KPSS tests: Tests each variable for stationarity (whether the time series has constant statistical properties)

ADF tests for unit roots (non-stationarity)

KPSS tests for trend stationarity

Log transformation: Applies natural log to all variables to stabilize variance

Z-score standardization: Only applied to money market fund NAV after log transformation

Re-testing: Runs stationarity tests again on log-transformed data

3. Lag Selection

AutoRegression modeling: Uses AIC/BIC criteria to find optimal lag length for the dependent variable (bond subscription amount)

Tests 1-12 lags: Appropriate for monthly data to capture seasonal patterns

4. Distributed Lag Model (DLM)

Multi-variable DLM: Models bond subscription as function of 6 explanatory variables with 2-period lags

Lag structure: Creates variables for current period (t), 1-period lag (t-1), and 2-period lag (t-2)

OLS regression: Fits the model using ordinary least squares

5. Results & Visualization

Model diagnostics: Saves coefficients, statistics, and diagnostic tests (normality, autocorrelation)

Lag impact visualization: Creates bar charts showing how each variable's impact varies across lag periods

Coefficient heatmap: Shows the strength and direction of relationships across variables and lags

Cumulative impact ranking: Ranks variables by their total effect across all lag periods
